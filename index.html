Working prototype. Published stories are static and read-only. New visitor entries are submitted privately through Netlify Forms for staff review; they do not appear publicly until approved and added to the published story file.

Where the voices come from

Every account here belongs to a real, named person and was published by a named outlet. Each is retold in the exhibit's own words with a link back to the original — these are summaries, not licensed reproductions. Reporting by the Tennessee Register, TIME, the USO, the Associated Press, The World (PRX), Connecting Vets, American Forces Press Service, and Military.com, 2011–2025.

Before any of this goes on a public screen, comms should either link out to each original or secure permission from the publisher — and ideally from the veteran.

A note on density

Most years on this timeline currently show one or two voices, or none. That is honest: it reflects what we have actually sourced, not what the exhibit could hold. The mockups showed a dozen or more per year. Filling the line is the collection work, and it is the main thing standing between this prototype and a finished exhibit.

On the pre-2001 voices

Six accounts belong to people already in uniform that morning: a Green Beret who had been planning to retire, a Night Stalker pilot, a Guard officer doing one weekend a month, a warrant officer training in Saudi Arabia, an Army chaplain from Ghana stationed in Germany, and an eighteen-year-old who volunteered for the Air Force on September 10th.

They matter because they are the only people on this line who did not choose. Everyone after 2001 decided something. This group had it decided for them — and then absorbed twenty years of consequence. For a Military and Veterans Center audience, they are likely a large share of the room.

Getting our own voices

  • Veterans History Project (Library of Congress) — over 121,000 collections as of May 2025, more than 96,000 online. Note: copyright stays with the veteran, so it's a place to find people, not to take clips from freely.
  • Voices of Veterans (Texas General Land Office) — Texas narrators, World War II through the War on Terror.
  • StoryCorps Military Voices Initiative — short, well-produced audio.
  • On campus and nearby: UTD's student veteran community, DFW-area VFW and American Legion posts, and interviews we record at the event.

Design decisions worth defending

  • Built for a bright room. Pale stone rather than dark, because a dark screen under Student Union lighting becomes a mirror. Still needs testing on the actual display in the actual room.
  • Not in UTD green and orange. Memorial exhibits conventionally step outside institutional branding, the same way a plaque isn't set in a university's marketing palette.
  • No veteran / non-veteran gate. Six paths, everyone gets one. Asking people to self-identify as a veteran at the door turns some of them away.
  • Two different questions. People who enlisted after are asked what made them raise their hand. People already in are asked what changed — "why did you join" is the wrong question for someone who joined in 1976.
  • Everything is skippable. Recounting why you enlisted, at a 9/11 memorial, can surface more than someone expected.

Still needed

  • A staff-owned moderation workflow for reviewing Netlify submissions and moving approved entries into the published story file.
  • Nothing user-submitted should reach a public screen unreviewed. The current build stores new entries privately and requires a separate approval step.
  • Written permissions for any account shown at length.
  • A staffed table rather than an unattended kiosk.
  • Accessibility pass: screen reader, large-text mode, seated-height screen.
